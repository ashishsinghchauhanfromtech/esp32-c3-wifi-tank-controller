
# 3D Printing & Manufacturing Notes

## Project
**ESP32-C3 WiFi Tank Controller — Mechanical Prototype**

## 1. Manufacturing Overview

The mechanical components of this tank prototype were designed in Tinkercad and fabricated using FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) 3D printing.

The printed components include:
- Chassis
- Gears
- Motor mounts

The objective was to create a functional mechanical structure capable of supporting the drive system and integrating with the electronic control hardware.

## 3. Design-to-Manufacturing Workflow

### Step 1 — CAD Modeling
Created the chassis, gears, and motor mounts using Tinkercad. The models were intended to provide structural support, motor mounting, and mechanical drive functionality.

### Step 2 — STL Export
Exported the designed components into STL format for preparation in the slicing software.

### Step 3 — Slicing and Print Preparation
Imported the STL models into Ultimaker Cura and prepared the components for fabrication on the Creality Ender 3.

The confirmed infill density was 100%. Other slicing parameters, including layer height and wall settings, have not been documented.

### Step 4 — Fabrication
Manufactured the mechanical components using PLA+ filament and FDM 3D printing.

### Step 5 — Assembly and Integration
Assembled the printed chassis, gears, and motor mounts as part of the tank prototype. The mechanical structure was integrated with the DC motors and electronic control system.

### Step 6 — Fit and Functional Evaluation
Evaluated the printed components during assembly and prototype testing. Several issues were identified, as documented below.

## 4. Printing and Assembly Observations

### Gear Fracture During Support Removal
A gear fractured at its central region during support removal, while its teeth remained intact.

**Observation:** The component was vulnerable during post-processing.

**Potential areas for investigation:**
- Hub geometry and surrounding wall thickness
- Support placement and removal technique
- Part orientation during printing
- Material behavior and local stress concentration

**Possible future improvements:** Review the hub geometry, adjust support placement, and evaluate alternative orientations or stronger local geometry.

### Gear Fit and Tolerance
Gear engagement and fit presented tightness or looseness issues.

**Observation:** The fit between mating components required attention during assembly.

**Potential areas for investigation:**
- Gear dimensions and center distance
- Shaft and bore dimensions
- Manufacturing tolerances
- Printer dimensional accuracy

**Possible future improvements:** Use small test prints to evaluate clearances before manufacturing the complete set of components.

### Stringing or Warping
Stringing or warping was observed during printing.

**Potential areas for investigation:**
- Retraction settings
- Nozzle and bed temperatures
- Bed adhesion
- Cooling settings
- Filament condition and storage

**Possible future improvements:** Calibrate retraction, verify material-specific temperature recommendations, and review bed preparation and first-layer adhesion.

### Motor Mount Alignment
Motor mounting alignment presented an issue during assembly.

**Observation:** The relationship between the mounting geometry and the motor position required attention.

**Potential areas for investigation:**
- Mounting-hole dimensions
- Motor seating surfaces
- Shaft alignment
- Clearance between the motor, gears, and chassis

**Possible future improvements:** Refine mounting geometry and validate alignment using a small test print before printing revised full-size parts.

*The potential causes and improvements listed above are design considerations for future iterations, not confirmed root causes or completed modifications.*

## 5. Design Considerations for Future Iterations

Potential areas for refinement include:

- Improving gear hub strength and resistance to handling damage
- Refining gear clearances and shaft fit
- Improving motor mounting alignment
- Reducing printing defects through slicer calibration
- Reviewing support placement for easier post-processing
- Evaluating material usage and structural requirements
- Improving assembly access and maintainability

Any revised geometry or print settings should be validated through physical testing.
